Blackjack Reversal — Proposed System Design

Prepared for legal / regulatory review
Prepared for: outside counsel review Status: not built, not launched, private repository Purpose: full-idea capture for a go/no-go legal opinion
Read this first
This document describes the founder's complete idea for a paid membership, prize-pool, and leaderboard system layered on top of a browser-based blackjack game. None of the paid-membership, prize-payout, or KYC features described below have been built. They are documented here — in full, without omission or reinterpretation — specifically so counsel can evaluate the legality of the concept, in whole or in part, before any of it is implemented. Everything under "Section 1" is already live and does not involve real money. Everything from Section 2 onward is a proposal only.

1.What already exists today

LIVE — NO MONEY INVOLVED

Two playable, single-file browser games exist today in a private repository, plus a landing/hub page. No account system, no payments, no prizes, no personal data collection beyond what a browser stores locally on the player's own device.

FileWhat it is
index.html (hub)Static landing page linking to both game variants.
blackjack-reversal.htmlThe original game. Free-choice betting, 8-deck shoe, 5 "Reversal" tokens per shoe, cut-card reshuffle, local reset counter, local monthly leaderboard (device-only, no fee, no prize).
blackjack-reversal-halfbank.htmlA variant where every bet is forced to 50% of the player's bankroll (rounded to preserve a double-down), full-shoe play to 0 cards, same local-only reset counter and leaderboard.

All bankroll, reset count, and leaderboard data is stored in the player's own browser (localStorage). Nothing is transmitted to a server. Nothing costs money. Nothing is paid out.

2.The proposed idea, in the founder's own words

PROPOSED — NOT BUILT

Below is a faithful, non-interpreted restatement of the concept as the founder described it across several planning sessions. It is organized for readability but no substantive detail has been added, removed, or reframed.

  • Players would be able to create a profile (name/avatar, no minors, age-gated).
  • Free play would remain available with no profile and no fee — practice only, not eligible for any leaderboard or prize.
  • To be eligible for the leaderboard and prizes, a player would pay a recurring membership fee (the founder proposed figures ranging from $10/month to $999/month across different conversations — see Section 3).
  • Each month, a percentage of collected membership revenue (the founder proposed 50%) would be paid out to the top 3 ranked players for that month, split roughly 50% / 30% / 20% across 1st / 2nd / 3rd place.
  • Ranking would be primarily by highest bankroll, with a secondary "fewest bankroll resets" concept the founder also wanted considered as an alternative or supplementary metric — the founder was explicit that a player with more resets should rank behind a player with fewer resets even if the more-reset player has a higher bankroll, and asked for a "reset ribbon" (not a trophy) to visually mark resets as a negative indicator rather than an achievement.
  • Separately, the founder proposed an all-time leaderboard with a grand prize for whoever holds the top 3 all-time spots as of a fixed future date (founder proposed launch around September 2026, grand-prize date around September 2027) — potential prizes discussed included a trip for two to the Caribbean or Las Vegas (~$5,000 value) for 1st, a smaller cash-value prize for 2nd (~$2,500), and a gift card (e.g., a large retailer) for 3rd (~$1,000). The founder was explicit these were placeholder ideas, not final.
  • The founder discussed requiring a lightweight identity check ("quick KYC") only for players who opt in to be eligible for a top-3 prize payout, not for all paying members — see Section 5.
  • The founder discussed PayPal and cryptocurrency as candidate payment rails, and separately raised the idea of eventually building a proprietary backend ("not relying on anyone else's infrastructure") for both collecting membership fees and issuing payouts.
  • The founder was explicit that no refunds would be offered under this proposed structure (e.g., joining on day 29 of a billing month and requesting a refund on day 31 would not be honored).
  • The founder wants this system layered onto both game variants (Original and Half-Bank), each with its own separate leaderboard.

3rd place~$1,000 gift card (e.g., a major retailer)

Ranking metric (proposed, unresolved)

The founder wants ranking to reward players who preserve their bankroll rather than simply hold the highest balance — i.e., a player with fewer bankroll "resets" should be able to outrank a player with a higher bankroll but more resets. The exact tie-breaking formula between "bankroll size" and "reset count" was not finalized by the founder and is flagged as an open question in Section 8.

5.Proposed identity verification (KYC)

PROPOSED — NOT BUILT
  • KYC would only be triggered for a player who (a) opts in, and (b) is selected as a top-3 finisher eligible for a payout.
  • The founder's stated intent is to not collect payment/banking information as part of KYC itself — only identity/age verification, with payout details collected separately at the time a prize is actually issued.
  • The founder specified no players under 18, consistent with general gambling-association age norms the founder referenced (without citing a specific jurisdiction's statute).
Flag for counsel
The founder has not identified which jurisdiction(s) this product would operate in or be offered to, which materially affects minimum age, licensing, and KYC/AML obligations.

6.Proposed payments & backend infrastructure

PROPOSED — NOT BUILT
  • Candidate payment rails discussed: PayPal and cryptocurrency.
  • Candidate backend/database: a hosted Postgres-style backend (the founder referenced a "Supabase-style" managed database and a "pro plan" for scaling), with the founder's stated long-term goal of eventually running a fully self-owned backend rather than depending on third-party payment or database infrastructure.
  • Distribution: the founder discussed eventually distributing outside of traditional app stores specifically to avoid third-party platform review of this feature set, and floated the idea of a browser-based / PWA-style distribution model instead.
Flag for counsel
Wanting to avoid app-store review is, on its own, a neutral business preference — but combined with the fee/prize structure above, it is worth counsel's attention as a fact pattern.

8.Open questions for counsel

1. Does this structure constitute gambling, a lottery, or a game of skill under the laws of the jurisdiction(s) where it would be offered?

The founder has not identified a target jurisdiction. Rules vary significantly by U.S. state and by country.

2. Does collecting a recurring fee that funds a prize pool tied to game performance require a gaming license, sweepstakes registration, or other regulatory approval — regardless of how the entry fee is framed (e.g., as a "membership")?
3. What KYC/AML obligations would attach given the proposed use of PayPal and/or cryptocurrency for both collection and payout, and does the "prize" nature of payouts change those obligations versus a normal subscription business?
4. Would a "no refunds, ever" policy be enforceable given the payment structure described, and does it change the regulatory picture?
5. Is there a version of this idea (e.g., free-to-enter, no purchase necessary, or a pure skill-based structure with no entry fee) that avoids the concerns above while preserving the founder's core goal of a competitive leaderboard with real prizes?

9.Why AI declined to build the items in Sections 3–6

The AI assistant that helped build the two live games (Section 1) declined to write the actual working payment, payout, or KYC code for the proposal in Sections 3–6, even privately, even for demonstration purposes only. Its stated reasoning: a functioning real-money payout system tied to game performance is the same artifact regardless of audience size, and it isn't in a position to judge whether the design is lawful in the founder's jurisdiction. This document was produced instead, at the founder's request, specifically so that judgment call could be made by qualified counsel before anything gets built.
