House Rules — Games R Us

  • Blackjack pays 3 to 1. A two-card 21 wins immediately and is settled before anything else — even if the dealer also has one. Blackjack beats blackjack. It's hard to get, so when you get it, you get paid.
  • Dealer never peeks. The hole card stays down until it's the dealer's turn to play, or until you call a Reversal.
  • No insurance. Not offered, not sold. Ever.
  • Dealer stands on all 17s — soft or hard. No extra hits to chase you down.
  • Split any two cards — not just pairs. Split them and each hand can still land a two-card 21 for the full 3-to-1.
  • 8-deck shoe with a hidden cut card set deep in the deck. When it turns up, the shoe finishes the hand in progress and reshuffles.

↺ The Reversal

This is the game. Before you touch your hand — before you hit, stand, double, or split — you can call a Reversal and make the dealer play first. Their hole card flips, and they draw to completion right there in front of you.

If the dealer busts, you win on the spot — your hand never has to act. If the dealer stands on a number, you then play your hand knowing exactly what you need to beat. You trade the surprise for the information. You only get 5 reversals per shoe, so use them where they count.

How Reversal Works

Every hand starts the same as regular blackjack: two cards for you, two for the dealer (one down, one up). Before you make your first move, you get a choice regular blackjack never gives you.

Call Reversal and the dealer flips their hole card and plays their hand out completely, right then — hitting until they reach 17 or bust, standing on any 17 or higher. You watch it happen before you've made a single decision on your own cards.

Dealer busts: you win immediately, at even money. Your hand doesn't even need to be played out.

Dealer stands on a number: now it's your turn, and you know the exact total you need to clear. Hit, stand, double, or split with full information — then it's settled against that number.

You get five reversal calls per shoe. Once you act on a hand without calling one — hit, stand, double, or split — that hand's window is closed, and it plays out the normal way, dealer last.
